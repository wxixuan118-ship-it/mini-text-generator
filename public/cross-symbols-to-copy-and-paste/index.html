Cross Symbols FAQ

What is the difference between a Latin cross and a Greek cross?

A Latin cross (✝) has a longer vertical arm than horizontal — it is the shape most people associate with Christianity. A Greek cross (✚) has four equal arms and is the form used on medical and first-aid symbols, as well as Byzantine and Orthodox iconography.

Why do some crosses appear in colour while others stay black?

Characters such as ❌ and ➕ were defined as emoji, so every platform draws them in colour. Characters such as ✝ ☦ ✚ are text symbols and take the colour of the surrounding text. Adding the variation selector — the ✝️ button above — asks for the colour version instead.

Which cross symbol is the official Christian one?

There is no single “official” cross across all traditions. ✝ U+271D, named LATIN CROSS, is the most widely recognised in Western Christianity. ☦ is the three-barred Orthodox cross, ☩ is the Jerusalem or Crusader cross, ✠ is the eight-pointed Maltese cross, and 🕈 is the ringed Celtic cross.

What does the dagger † mean in typography?

The dagger, also called an obelus, marks a second footnote on a page — the asterisk (*) is first, the dagger † is second, and the double dagger ‡ is third. Printers have used it this way since the sixteenth century.

How were cross symbols added to Unicode?

Most religious and decorative crosses entered Unicode through the Dingbats block (U+2700–U+27BF) in version 1.0, and later additions like the Celtic cross 🕈 arrived in Unicode 7.0. The Coptic crosses come from the Coptic block, added in Unicode 4.1.

Can I use cross symbols in formal documents and academic papers?

Absolutely — the dagger † and double dagger ‡ are standard footnote marks in academic publishing, and they appear in every professional font. For religious or historical writing, the Latin ✝ and Greek ✚ crosses are safe choices because they exist in nearly every Unicode font.
