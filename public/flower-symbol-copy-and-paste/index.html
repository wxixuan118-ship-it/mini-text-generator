Flower Symbol FAQ

What is floriography and how do flower symbols fit in?

Floriography is the Victorian “language of flowers” — a system where specific blooms carry specific meanings, used to send coded messages through bouquets. While Unicode flower emoji and text symbols are not strictly part of that tradition, people naturally assign floriographic meanings to them: 🌹 for love, 🌻 for loyalty, 🌸 for fleeting beauty, and so on.

What is the difference between a text floret and a flower emoji?

Text florets like ✿ ❀ ✾ come from the Dingbats and Miscellaneous Symbols blocks and behave like letters — they are black by default and inherit your font colour. Flower emoji like 🌸 🌹 🌺 were designed as full-colour pictures and always render in colour, no matter what font you use. Florets blend into text; emoji stand out from it.

Which flower symbol means love in the language of flowers?

The red rose 🌹 is the classic symbol of romantic love and passion — it held that position in Victorian floriography and still does today. Tulips 🌷 also carry romantic meaning (perfect love), while the cherry blossom 🌸 represents more transient, delicate beauty rather than enduring passion.

Do different cultures assign different meanings to flowers?

Absolutely. The lotus 🪷 is sacred in Buddhism and Hinduism, symbolising purity and enlightenment. The chrysanthemum represents longevity in China but mourning in some European countries. Even the rose varies — yellow roses mean friendship in some cultures and jealousy in others. When in doubt, context matters most.

Why are there so few text-based flower symbols?

Most decorative text flowers come from the Dingbats block (originally a 1970s Zapf Dingbats typeface) and the Miscellaneous Symbols block. Unicode added far more flower emoji than text flowers because emoji were designed for visual communication, whereas text symbols evolved from printer's ornaments and mathematical notation.

What does the cherry blossom symbolize in Japanese culture?

In Japan, the cherry blossom — sakura — represents the fleeting nature of life, beauty and transience. The blossoms fall after just a week or two, a reminder that loveliness is brief and should be savoured. It is also deeply associated with spring, new beginnings and academic or career starts.
