Bow Symbol FAQ

What is the difference between → the text arrow and ➤ the heavy arrowhead?

→ comes from the Arrows Unicode block and was designed for mathematical and text use — it is thin, light and matches the body text weight. ➤ is a Dingbats character with a solid, heavy triangular head that stands out on a page, making it the go-to for bullet points and UI elements.

Why is 🏹 in colour but → is black and white?

🏹 was defined as an emoji from the start, so every platform draws it as a full-colour picture with shading and details. → is a text symbol from the Arrows block and inherits the colour of whatever font surrounds it — black by default, but it can be any colour your CSS or app sets.

Which arrow symbol is best for a resume or bullet list?

For professional documents, stick with → or ➔ — they are clean, widely supported and read as standard punctuation. For marketing copy or social media, ➤ or ➜ have more visual weight and draw the eye down a list.

Why are there so many right arrow styles?

Unicode inherited arrows from decades of character sets — mathematical arrows from ISO standards, Zapf Dingbats arrows from print, and UI arrows from early computing. Each style was designed for a different context, from mathematical implication ⇒ to map directions ➡.

Do bow and arrow symbols work in gaming usernames?

Most games that allow Unicode in display names accept 🏹 and the arrow symbols. Competitive shooters and archery games in particular love the bow and arrow emoji. Test a quick copy-paste into the name field first — if it shows up, you are good to go.

What does the bullseye ◎ symbol mean in Unicode?

The bullseye or concentric circles symbol comes from the Geometric Shapes block and officially goes by “bullseye.” It serves double duty online — as a literal target for archery and goal themes, and as a stylised “on point” or “perfect match” marker in captions and bios.
